# The Grill — Team 6, Round 1

> This is a stress-test, not a status report. The build is done; these are the six decisions still hanging over demo day and what comes after. Each question has a recommended call. Drop this in Discord, everybody weighs in, majority or lead-builder call settles it. A settled answer unlocks the next round of questions; an ignored one becomes a surprise on stage.

**Q1 - Demo roles:** Who does what during the 2 minutes and the Q&A after? The script assumes one voice (Morgan), but someone should drive the clicks so the speaker never looks down, and someone should own Q&A on the data ("is that real?").
**My call:** Morgan speaks, one person drives with the answer key, Spenser takes build questions, whoever researched the funding side takes program questions. Never make the speaker also the driver.

**Q2 - Put it on a real URL before demos?** Right now the app only exists as a file. GitHub Pages on the public repo would give `index.html` a live URL in about two minutes, which means a QR code on screen and the whole room running it on their phones during your demo. That is the difference between "watch us" and "try it right now."
**My call:** Yes, turn on GitHub Pages and put the QR up during the close. A room full of people taking the fit test on their own phones while judges deliberate is the strongest closing argument available, and it costs nothing.

**Q3 - What happens after today?** The wrap-up literally asks "where this goes next." Options: (a) hand it to a civic operator (a workforce board, CPS counselors, CWIT) and offer to keep maintaining it, (b) keep building as a team side project with a monthly data-verification pass, (c) one-neighborhood pilot with a named partner, (d) leave it as open source and let it find its owner.
**My call:** (a) with (d) as the floor. This product's natural owner is an institution that already talks to the audience daily. The team's ask at wrap-up should be one sentence: "we want an intro to anyone at a workforce board or CPS counseling." A specific ask beats a vague vision.

**Q4 - Who owns this?** It lives on one member's GitHub under MIT, built by six people who met this morning. If it stays a civic gift, MIT and shared credit is perfect. If anyone ever monetizes any piece of it (the paid pairing idea), today's ambiguity becomes tomorrow's fight.
**My call:** Say out loud in Discord: "MIT, open source, team credit in the README, nobody owns it, anyone can fork." One message, everyone thumbs-up, settled forever. If a commercial arm ever forms, it starts as a new conversation with the whole team, not a quiet assumption.

**Q5 - The $2,000 pairing pitch: keep or cut for demo day?** The try-a-trade marketplace is the most original idea in the deck and also the only unbuilt one, and it invites liability questions (insurance, job-site safety, who holds the money) that can eat your Q&A alive.
**My call:** Cut it from the 2-minute demo entirely; keep it as ONE sentence in the wrap-up ("the long arc is paid try-a-trade months with working tradespeople"). Demo day rewards what runs. The built thing is strong enough to stand alone, and this is the scope-cut this round.

**Q6 - The staleness liability:** the app's authority rests on program data verified in August 2026 and union windows that change monthly. A wrong deadline told to a real person is worse than no app. Does the team commit to (a) a "last verified" date visible on every program card, (b) a monthly re-verification pass with a named owner, and (c) one disclaimer line ("verify with the provider before you commit money or quit anything")?
**My call:** All three, and (a) before any workforce board sees it. The app already says "call before you count on it" in spots; make the date visible and this weakness becomes your credibility story: "every card tells you how fresh it is."
