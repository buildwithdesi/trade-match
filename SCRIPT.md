# Trade Match — 2-Minute Demo Script

> Written to be read aloud. Reads at about 2:00 at demo pace.
> Setup: open `trade-match.html` full screen on the intro page. Answer key at the bottom.
> The promise underneath every beat: **we're going to help you find a job, and make you better at it.**

---

## The script

**[0:00 — the fear]**

"Maya is 17. She's a senior at a Chicago public school. And she's scared.

Her whole life, she's been told the same thing: work hard, go to college, get a good job.

Then she watched AI write better essays than her AP English class. She watched it code. Her cousin graduated last spring with a marketing degree, sent out 300 applications, and heard almost nothing back.

So Maya is doing the math every night at 2 a.m.: take on $80,000 in debt to train for a job that might not exist in four years.

That's not a plan. That's a gamble. And she knows it."

*(Pause two beats. Let the room sit in it.)*

**[0:30 — the turn]**

"Now here's what nobody has shown her.

This city is aging. The bridges, the water mains, the wiring, the elevators. Most of it was built before Maya's parents were born, and the people who keep it running are retiring faster than we're replacing them. Chicago doesn't just have jobs for Maya. Chicago needs Maya.

An electrician here can make six figures. An elevator technician, over 100k. AI can write an essay. It can't fix a broken elevator.

But Maya has no idea where to start. She's never held a power tool. She doesn't know which trade fits her, what it pays, or who to call.

That's what we built Trade Path to solve. Our promise to her is simple: we're going to help you find the job, and we're going to make you better at it."

**[0:55 — the demo]**

"First, she answers 20 questions about how she actually works, not what she knows. Is she afraid of heights? Is she okay with grease? Does she work well with people? Trade Path ranks her fit across 16 trades.

Then we make it real. She sees apprenticeship opportunities, companies hiring, and tradespeople in her neighborhood who can help her get started. This is built on the city's own data, and it's built to plug into the partners who already do this well: the city, the union training programs, the schools. We're not replacing that path. We're the front door to it.

Her first job is no longer an abstraction. It has a name. A neighborhood. A person to call.

And before she commits to trade school, or years and thousands of dollars on the wrong path, she can spend a month on the job with a real Chicago tradesperson. She tries the work before she commits to it. If she loves it, she moves forward with confidence. If she doesn't, she finds out before it costs her.

Then comes the part nobody expects. The same AI that scared her becomes her tool. It quizzes her for the licensing exam. It's the guide in her pocket on the job site. It helps her trace a fault she's never seen before. We're not just helping Maya get away from AI. We're putting it in her toolbelt, so she's the sharpest apprentice on the crew."

**[1:45 — the close]**

"So when Chicago asks, 'What can we do for our kids?' This is one answer.

Five minutes. That's all it takes to turn 'what's going to happen to me?' into 'here's what I'm going to do.'"
